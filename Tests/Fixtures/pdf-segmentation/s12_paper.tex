\begin{abstract}
Automated manuscript verification tools frequently confuse topical keyword overlap with semantic entailment. We study geometric passage extraction and numeric-unit alignment over two-column scientific documents, evaluating 368 regression tests and 1,240 benchmark queries on local PDF archives.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Scientific authors routinely cite quantitative findings from multi-column archival PDFs. However, naive extraction pipelines merge running headers with body paragraphs and misclassify section boundaries when numbering or wording deviates from narrow templates.

Our deterministic verifier executes without any neural network components and completes static analysis across 368 regression tests in 14.5 ms on a single CPU core. Unlike probabilistic language models, rule-based lexical matching only locates candidate passages and checks explicit numbers.

\section{Related Work}
Prior citation checkers validate bibliographic metadata against Crossref and OpenAlex registries, confirming that DOIs and publication years exist. Metadata validation alone cannot detect when an author cites a real paper but transcribes 9,999 tests instead of 368 tests, or writes seconds instead of milliseconds.

Existing PDF indexers also treat single-word hits in SQLite FTS5 disjunction queries as topical grounding, causing unrelated statements that happen to share one common English word to receive positive relevance scores.

\section*{3. Methodology}
We segment each PDF page into positioned text lines using PDFKit selection bounds and partition full-width spanning blocks from left and right columns. Candidate passages are indexed in SQLite FTS5 with Porter stemming and evaluated against explicit numeric-unit pairs.

\section{Experimental Evaluation}
Across 1,240 benchmark queries, memory consumption remained 48 MB while achieving 94.2\% precision and a 3.2x throughput speedup ($p < 0.001$). Dataset preparation required 2.5 hours across 18 distinct conference templates.

On pages dominated by wide spanning tables, leaving fewer than three body lines in either column causes threshold-based column detectors to fall back to single-column vertical sorting, interleaving left and right column phrases.

\section{Conclusion}
Separating repeating margin furniture and spanning floats from two-column body streams prevents reading-order corruption, while pairing numbers with their governing units or nouns eliminates silent magnitude errors.
